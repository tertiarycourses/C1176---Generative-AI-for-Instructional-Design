# Meridian Retail Group — Training Brief

> **This is a fictional organisation created for the C1176 course.** All names,
> details and figures are invented for practice. Use this brief as the source
> material for the course you design, or substitute your own **non-confidential**
> course.

## The organisation in one line

**Meridian Retail Group** runs a small chain of **neighbourhood lifestyle stores**
across Singapore — everyday homeware, gifts and lifestyle products — known for
friendly, personal service in the heartlands.

## The business need (why this course exists)

- Meridian is **hiring a wave of new frontline retail associates** to staff new and expanding stores.
- Many new hires are **first-time workers** (students, career changers) with little or no retail experience.
- Store managers report that new associates are **keen but nervous**: they hesitate to approach customers, freeze when a customer complains, and are unsure when to call a supervisor.
- Customer feedback scores dip in the weeks after a new intake, then recover — Meridian wants new associates **confident and consistent from week one**.

**The request:** design a short, practical onboarding course — **"Delivering Great
Customer Service at Meridian"** — that new associates complete in their **first week**.

## The learners (the audience)

- **New frontline retail associates** in their first week at Meridian.
- Age range broad (roughly 17–55); many are first-time or early-career workers.
- **Prior knowledge:** little to none about retail service; comfortable with phones and apps but not with workplace training.
- **Motivation:** want to do well and not embarrass themselves; short attention for long lectures; respond to practical, hands-on learning.
- **Constraints:** limited time (this sits inside a busy onboarding week); mixed reading confidence, so materials must be plain and visual.

## The course constraints

| Constraint | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | About **3 hours** total |
| **Mode** | **Blended** — a facilitator-led workshop plus a short self-paced e-learning module and take-away job aids |
| **Group size** | 8–12 new associates per workshop |
| **Delivery** | In-store or at the training room; facilitator is an experienced store supervisor, not a professional trainer — so the facilitator guide must be easy to run |
| **Take-aways** | Learners keep **job aids** (a checklist and a quick-reference card) to use on the shop floor |

## What good looks like (the performance the course must build)

After the course, a new associate should be able to:

- **Greet** a customer warmly and offer help in a natural, non-pushy way.
- **Understand** a customer's need by listening and asking simple questions.
- **Handle a complaint** calmly using a simple **service-recovery method** (a short, memorable set of steps), staying empathetic under pressure.
- **Know when to escalate** — recognise the situations a supervisor must handle, and hand over well.
- Reflect Meridian's friendly, **neighbourhood-store** service style throughout.

> **Note on the service-recovery method:** the course uses a simple, memorable
> set of steps (for example *Listen → Acknowledge → Apologise → Act → Follow up*).
> You may name and shape the exact steps as you design the course — keep them
> short and easy to remember under pressure.

## Constraints on content (responsible use)

- There are **no real customer records or employee data** to use — and you must not invent any. Keep examples generic and realistic.
- Any **Meridian-specific policy** (refund limits, escalation contacts, uniform standards) is **not in this brief** — leave a clear placeholder where the real policy would go, to be confirmed with a Meridian subject expert before delivery.
- Keep all examples **fair and inclusive** of both customers and staff.

## Suggested shape (you will design your own in Labs 3–5)

A possible flow — you will derive your own from the objectives:

1. Welcome & why service matters at Meridian
2. Knowing the customer — greeting and understanding needs
3. Handling complaints — the service-recovery method
4. Knowing when to escalate
5. Putting it together — practice and wrap-up
